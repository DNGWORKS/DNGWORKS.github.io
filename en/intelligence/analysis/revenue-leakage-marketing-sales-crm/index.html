7 revenue leaks between Marketing, Sales and CRM

A funnel rarely breaks in one place. Revenue usually leaks through small operational gaps in lead definition, response speed, handoff, follow-up and measurement.

Author
Dũng Nguyễn
Published
Jul 31, 2026
9 min read

Đồ hoạ biên tập DNGWORKS

Leak 1: Marketing and Sales use different definitions

Marketing may count any form submission as a lead, while Sales only considers a prospect with a real problem, budget and buying timeline to be qualified. When both teams use different definitions, the top of the funnel looks healthy while the bottom disappoints.

Create one shared definition covering fit, problem, intent, contactability and the next action. Each criterion should be observable rather than subjective.

Leaks 2–3: Slow response and unclear handoff

Leads arrive without an owner, response deadline or handoff status. They are called twice, forgotten or contacted only when a salesperson has spare time.

Assign an owner as soon as the lead enters the system. The SLA can vary by industry and channel, but it must be explicit and measurable.

Leaks 4–5: No follow-up rhythm and no lost-reason data

One failed call does not mean there is no demand. Build a follow-up sequence across time, channel and message. Every closed lead should also carry a standardized reason such as poor fit, wrong timing, no budget, competitor selected or unreachable.

If lost reasons are not captured, the company cannot tell whether to fix targeting, the offer or sales execution.

Leaks 6–7: Vanity metrics and no feedback loop

Impressions, clicks and form submissions only describe the top of the funnel. Revenue management requires contact rate, qualification rate, proposal rate, win rate, conversion time and revenue by source.

Sales must then return lead-quality feedback to Marketing. Without that loop, Marketing keeps optimizing for cheap leads instead of revenue-producing leads.

Seven-day action checklist

  1. Agree on one qualified-lead definition.
  2. Assign owners and SLAs by source.
  3. Standardize six to ten pipeline stages.
  4. Build at least five follow-up touches.
  5. Standardize lost reasons.
  6. Connect lead source to revenue.
  7. Run a weekly 30-minute Marketing–Sales data review.

Evidence note

An operating framework synthesized by Dũng Nguyễn. No unverified performance claims are used.

Key takeaways

  • Define a qualified lead before increasing ad spend.
  • Every lead needs an owner, a response SLA and a next action.
  • The dashboard must connect lead source, sales stage and revenue.
