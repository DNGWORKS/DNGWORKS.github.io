A 60-minute funnel audit: what to inspect first

A fast audit process for finding likely revenue bottlenecks before recommending more budget or tools.

Author
Dũng Nguyễn
Published
Jul 31, 2026
6 min read

Đồ hoạ biên tập DNGWORKS

0–10 minutes: define the commercial objective

Write down the product, customer, order value, buying cycle and revenue target. Without a clear product and timeframe, the analysis will drift.

10–25 minutes: read the pipeline

Count volume and conversion between lead, contacted, qualified, meeting, proposal and won. A clean table is enough to reveal the sharpest drop.

25–40 minutes: inspect offer and message

Check whether landing page, advertising, forms and sales scripts communicate the same promise. Review CTA clarity, proof and form friction.

40–50 minutes: inspect speed and ownership

Sample ten recent leads. Identify owner, response time, follow-up and where the outcome was recorded. This often reveals the gap between documented and actual process.

50–60 minutes: choose one priority hypothesis

Write: “If we fix X, metric Y is likely to improve because Z.” Assign an owner, deadline and success threshold. Do not end with thirty unordered problems.

A good audit does not create more slides. It clarifies the next decision.

Evidence note

A practical playbook, not a substitute for a full audit in complex environments.

Key takeaways

  • Start with revenue and pipeline, not follower count.
  • Find the largest constraint instead of fixing everything at once.
  • Every finding needs an owner, deadline and verification metric.
